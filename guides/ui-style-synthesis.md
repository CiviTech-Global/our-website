# Build on Swiss order, ration everything else

CiviTech Global should not blend its eight candidate styles evenly. The evidence supports a clear ranking. **Swiss modernism and signifier-rich minimalism** should be the structural base for every surface. **Motion** belongs in the system as tokens, split into a productive and an expressive set. **Organic/biophilic** design should enter mainly through photography and a heritage palette. **Claymorphism and neubrutalism** each contribute a narrow set of traits, used only on marketing and community surfaces: clay supplies illustration and a hero CTA, and neubrutalism supplies outline-strength focus and selection states. **Kinetic typography** is limited to whole-word Persian motion on marketing pages. **Neumorphism (Soft UI)** gets nothing beyond a pressed-state metaphor, because its shadow-only boundaries fail WCAG 1.4.11 by construction. The governing model is IBM Carbon's single token set with productive and expressive modes ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/typography/style-strategies/)), because the trust research points the same way. Lay users judge credibility mostly by visual polish: in the Stanford / Consumer WebWatch study, 46.1% of comments cited the "design look" ([ClickZ](https://clickz.com/study-for-site-cred-looks-matter/56564/)). NN/g's trust factors reward upfront disclosure and consistency over novelty ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). Persian-first RTL adds hard constraints that rule out several trend techniques: no letter-spacing, no per-letter animation, physical shadow offsets that do not mirror, and hierarchy that must come from weight because the script has no case. No published study tests any of these styles with Iranian users, so the recommendations below are evidence-informed design decisions that still need validation with Persian-speaking and low-vision users.

## Two styles are grammars, six are skins

The eight styles are not the same kind of thing. Swiss modernism and minimalism are systems of order that most modern design systems absorbed long ago. The other six are surface treatments with visible trend cycles. Treating them as equals is the main way a synthesis goes wrong.

### Swiss modernism supplies the grid, the scale and the neutrality

The International Typographic Style emerged in 1940s–50s Switzerland, through the Basel school (Hofmann, Ruder) and the Zurich school (Müller-Brockmann). It valued "cleanliness, legibility and impartiality" ([Ulster IXD](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/b00777404-ixd/?p=437)). Its technical signature was a **mathematical grid, flush-left sans-serif type and objective photography in place of illustration** ([Ulster IXD](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/b00813157-ixd/2022/01/11/international-typographic-style)). Müller-Brockmann's modular grids scale into hierarchical structures, though he warned that "the grid system is an aid, not a guarantee" ([madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=4700)). The style has a known weakness. After 1968 it was attacked as "cold, formal, dogmatic" ([Swiss National Library](https://www.nb.admin.ch/en/postmodern-posters-1970-to-today)), and Weingart broke it apart from inside the Basel school. That history warns against sterile execution, not against the principles themselves.

Two modern descendants matter for CiviTech. **IBM Carbon's 2x Grid** is the best-documented production Swiss grid. It builds on an 8px mini-unit, uses 4/8/16 columns across breakpoints from 320px to 1584px, keeps 16px padding everywhere, and allows gutterless grids for closely related content, which is what dense dashboards need ([Carbon 2x Grid](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/2x-grid/overview/)). The Swiss Federal Administration's **CD Bund** has governed all admin.ch sites since 2007. It is a mandatory, unmodifiable identity layer that "promotes the recognizability of federal websites and prevents costly uncontrolled growth in web design" ([Swiss Confederation Web Guidelines](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/swiss-styleguide@3.0.0/README.md)). CD Bund is the closest public-sector analogue to what CiviTech is building: a multilingual civic platform with one fixed identity layer and flexible components.

Swiss principles carry over to Persian, but some of them change. Flush-left becomes flush-right (start-aligned), and CSS Grid handles that automatically because grid lines number from the inline start. The Swiss habit of uppercase, tracked labels has no Persian equivalent. As the Suisse Int'l Arabic designers put it, "due to the absence of italics, caps, and small caps in the Arabic script, weight and proportional variations are crucial for creating complex visual hierarchy" ([Typographica](https://typographica.org/?p=22377)). Vazirmatn's variable weight axis, from 100 to 900, is the tool for that hierarchy ([Vazirmatn README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/rastikerdar/vazirmatn@32.0.0/README.md)).

### Minimalism earns its place only with strong signifiers

Minimalism traces to Dieter Rams' "less, but better" ([Vitsœ](https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/about/good-design)). NN/g's survey of 112 minimalist sites quantified its defining traits: flat textures (96%), a limited palette with one accent for clickable elements (95%), restricted features (87%), maximised negative space (84%) and dramatic typography (75%) ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/characteristics-minimalism/)). Its documented cost falls exactly on CiviTech's densest surfaces. In NN/g's eyetracking study, pages with **weak signifiers took 22% longer and produced 25% more fixations**. On one site, 86% of users reached the target link in the strong-signifier version versus 50% in the weak one. NN/g concluded that flat UI is workable only at low information density with high-contrast targets ([NN/g 2017](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/)). Admin dashboards and marketplace listings are the opposite case. CiviTech therefore needs "flat 2.0" minimalism, which keeps the restraint and adds back borders, fills, underlines and visible focus. NN/g also rejects ghost-button primaries and hamburger-only navigation on content-heavy sites, and warns that "minimalism for minimalism's sake alone doesn't help users" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/characteristics-minimalism/)).

### Neumorphism fails by construction; claymorphism survives as illustration

Neumorphism grew out of Alexander Plyuto's 2019 Dribbble banking concept and was popularised by Michał Malewicz ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/neumorphism-and-css/)). Each element shares the colour of its background, and depth comes only from paired light and dark shadows. That design choice is the flaw. WCAG treats a drop shadow as part of the adjacent colour, so the shadow cannot supply the 3:1 boundary that SC 1.4.11 requires ([W3C Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). Malewicz himself estimated that **80% of Dribbble neumorphic designs fail WCAG**. He said pressed and unpressed states "look the same", and he advised using the style on cards, not buttons ([Built In](https://builtin.com/design-ux/neumorphism-accessibility)). He later called it a "zombie trend" that "nobody's making any products with" ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/neumorphism)). By 2026 a vendor review found it lives only in "concept work and single-purpose instruments" and has "mostly died in production software that has to serve everyone" ([Setproduct](https://www.setproduct.com/blog/neumorphism-design-guide)). Adding a 3:1 border fixes the contrast failure, but then the element is no longer neumorphic.

Malewicz also coined claymorphism, which "inflates" the neumorphic shape ([HYPE4 Academy](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/claymorphism-in-user-interfaces)). Its decisive change is technical. It uses one outer shadow plus two inner shadows, which "allows for Claymorphism to have any background color, independent of the background" ([Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2022/03/claymorphism-css-ui-design-trend/)). That removes the structural contrast trap, but the style keeps two problems. Its puffy radii and padding waste density, and its tone risks a "slightly child-like interface." Smashing positions it as an enhancement to flat design for CTAs, toggles and cards, not a replacement. LogRocket's examples are mostly avatars, app icons and illustration sets such as Toy Faces ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/what-is-claymorphism-web-design/)). Both styles do have one legitimate evidential footing. NN/g's strong-signifier pages used shadows, gradients and slightly 3D buttons, so moderate depth on interactive elements improves findability. That supports soft elevation in general, not either rendering style in particular.

### Neubrutalism contributes outlines, not attitude

Neubrutalism descends from Pascal Deville's brutalistwebsites.com (2014), whose principle was "an attitude, not a visual style" ([Seattle Web Design](https://www.seattlewebdesign.com/blog/seattle-web-design-online-marketing-blog/post/the-strange-new-web-design-trend-you-may-not-have-noticed)). It became popular through Gumroad's 2021 rebrand and Figma's marketing. NN/g defines it by "high contrast, blocky layouts, bold colors, thick borders, and 'unpolished' elements" with "solid, single-color offset shadows" ([NN/g 2025](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/)). Typical values are 2–4px black borders and 4–8px zero-blur offsets ([madegooddesigns 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/)). The style is still widely available, for example in an open-source React/Tailwind v4 kit with 64 components and about 5.4k GitHub stars ([neobrutalism.dev](https://www.neobrutalism.dev/)). Commentators describe it as "cooling as a full-page style" in 2026, while bold CTAs inside conventional layouts still work ([ThePlusAddons](https://theplusaddons.com/blog/web-design-trends-2026/)). The 2026 practitioner advice is explicit: avoid it for financial services, which "rely on signals of stability", for healthcare, where users "may be anxious or vulnerable", and for government sites, which serve "diverse audiences needing neutral clarity" ([madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/)). That opinion is not experimental evidence, but it covers CiviTech's insurance, consultation and admin surfaces. What remains useful is its contrast vocabulary. A solid 2–3px outline meets 1.4.11 without effort and can serve as the system-wide focus and selection idiom.

### Organic/biophilic: photographs have evidence, blobs do not

Biophilic design comes from E.O. Wilson's 1984 biophilia hypothesis. It was formalised by Kellert in 2008 and by Terrapin Bright Green's 14 patterns in 2014 ([Terrapin](https://www.terrapinbrightgreen.com/reports/14-patterns/)). Terrapin's own evidence ratings matter here. Visual Connection with Nature and Prospect/Refuge receive the highest rating, while **Biomorphic Forms, the blobs and curves that "organic UI" usually means, receives the lowest**. The digital evidence follows the same pattern. A 2024 meta-analysis of 10 studies with 886 participants found that digital nature imagery produced stress recovery equivalent to physical nature with matched content (SMD −0.01), though risk of bias across the included studies ranged from low to high ([Fan & Baharum 2024](https://eprints.um.edu.my/46328/)). Richard Taylor's fractal research reports the lowest stress at mid-complexity patterns, around fractal dimension D 1.3–1.5 ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/fractal-patterns-nature-and-art-are-aesthetically-pleasing-and-stress-reducing-180962738/)). That argues for low-density girih or eslimi accents rather than dense ornament. The cultural anchor is strong. UNESCO describes the four-part chahar bagh as the "original principle of the Persian Garden", built on the right angle and geometric proportion ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1372-006)). This makes it an "ordered nature" that fits a Swiss grid better than generic Western wellness blobs. A 2020 thesis argues that Iranian digital products mostly "emulate western patterns" ([LSU](https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_theses/5172/)), so a garden-and-tile identity would also differentiate CiviTech. Headspace's 2024 identity offers a working rule: illustration for inner or abstract states and photography for real services ([Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2024/04/30/headspace/)).

### Motion is a system; kinetic type is a garnish Persian script constrains

Motion-driven design is the only one of the six skins that design systems have already codified. Carbon divides it into productive motion, which "creates a sense of efficiency and responsiveness" for button states, dropdowns and data tables, and expressive motion, which should be reserved "for occasional, important moments." Its duration tokens are 70, 110, 150, 240, 400 and 700ms, with separate easing curves for each mode ([Carbon motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/)). Material 3 adds spring tokens. Spatial springs, which move position and size, may overshoot slightly (damping 0.9), while effects springs, which change colour and opacity, are critically damped with no bounce ([Material Motion.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md)). NN/g sets useful durations at 100–500ms and warns that "it is far more common for animations to be too long than too short" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)).

Kinetic typography descends from Saul Bass's title sequences of the late 1950s ([Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/north-by-northwest)). Its best-documented modern brand use is Spotify Wrapped, which turns user data into animated copy ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/spotify-wrapped-campaign-identity-2022-graphic-design-301122)). In Persian it hits a hard technical wall. Splitting text into per-letter spans breaks cursive joining, and "there is no CSS that fixes it" ([Framer community](https://www.framer.com/community/posts/XLYzCtPVdM4AU4mFCUKu1F/)). Letter-spacing also breaks Arabic-script ligatures in a font-dependent way ([W3C www-style](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/www-style/2014Feb/0476.html)). Vazirmatn has only a weight axis, so Persian kinetic type reduces to animating whole words or lines, mask reveals, and weight morphs. Animating weight triggers layout, so it suits brief marketing moments only.

## Trust is earned by disclosure and restraint, not by novelty

The credibility research explains why the playful styles must stay away from money and identity flows. In the Stanford / Consumer WebWatch study, 2,684 consumers assessed 100 sites, and **46.1% of their comments cited "design look"**. Health and finance experts, by contrast, weighted named authors, citations and unbiased sourcing, and the study warned that consumers were "frequently distracted by superficial aspects" ([ClickZ](https://clickz.com/study-for-site-cred-looks-matter/56564/)). NN/g's four trust factors are design quality, upfront disclosure, correct and current content, and connection to the wider web. Its study gave concrete failures that map onto CiviTech's insurance and booking flows. One participant abandoned a service after **35 seconds because rates were not stated**. Forcing an address form before showing any content created a negative impression. Every participant read third-party reviews before hiring a provider ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). The aesthetic-usability effect cuts both ways. Kurosu and Kashimura's 252 participants rated attractive ATM layouts as easier to use, but attractiveness forgives only minor problems and can hide real ones during testing ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/)).

For a civic platform, the trust levers are therefore structural. They are explicit fees and statuses, findable legal-entity and contact details, browsing without a login wall, real photography of real people instead of idealised outcomes, and visible external validation. Polish matters, but it should come from consistent execution, not from novelty. Performance is a trust signal too: on filtered or slow Iranian connections, a failed web font or a stalled hero video reads as broken, and NN/g counts broken links against design quality. No sector-specific study of trust seals, badges or government plain style was retrieved, and no study has measured trust for neubrutalist or clay interfaces in insurance. The case against those styles in task flows rests on practitioner consensus plus the general credibility findings, not on a direct test.

## One token set, two intensities, is the only combination model with evidence

No NN/g or peer-reviewed study tests mixing several visual styles; the "hybrid" literature is trend blogs. The best evidence is architectural. **Carbon runs one token set with productive and expressive modes** in both typography and motion. The productive mode uses `01` type styles with fixed headings, suits forms and controls, and is judged by task time. The expressive mode uses `02` styles with fluid headings, suits exploration and reading, and is judged by click-through. The two blend on the same site at component level: productive in IBM.com's mega menu and filters, expressive in banners. Type styles stay consistent within any single component or task ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/typography/style-strategies/)). Material 3 Expressive (May 2025) is the strongest case for more expression. Google reports 46 studies with more than 18,000 participants, and expressive layouts helped users find key elements **up to 4x faster** ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=671309); [Phandroid](https://phandroid.com/2025/05/13/material-3-expressive-makes-google-apps-feel-less-robotic/)). These are Google's own numbers, reported through the press and not replicated. They suggest expression aids salience, but they do not justify playful styling in insurance forms.

The intensity switch should therefore follow the task, not the domain. A marketplace browse page is mostly productive with expressive category headers. A consultation landing page is expressive until the booking form opens, and from that point it is productive. The dominant-plus-accents hierarchy follows from the matrix. Swiss and minimalism carry layout, type and restraint everywhere. Motion is a token layer present in both modes at different speeds. Biophilic mood comes through palette and photography. Clay, neubrutalist and kinetic accents are confined to the expressive intensity, and each has a narrow job.

## The 2025–2026 toolchain makes this cheap to build

The engineering standards have matured enough that a two-intensity system no longer needs custom infrastructure. The **W3C DTCG format reached its first stable version, 2025.10, on 28 October 2025**. It has Format, Color and Resolver modules covering light/dark and accessibility theming, aliases, and OKLCH and other CSS Color 4 spaces ([W3C DTCG](https://www.w3.org/community/design-tokens/2025/10/28/design-tokens-specification-reaches-first-stable-version/)). It is a Community Group report, not a W3C Standard ([designtokens.org](https://www.designtokens.org/faq/)). Tailwind v4's `@theme` turns tokens into utilities and plain CSS variables, defines its default palette in OKLCH, and uses `@theme inline` to point semantic tokens at runtime-switchable variables ([Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/theme)). `oklch()` has been Baseline widely available since May 2023 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/oklch)). Relative colour syntax such as `oklch(from var(--brand) calc(l + 0.1) c h)` lets one accent token derive its hover, pressed and shadow tints. That matters for clay shadows, which have to be re-derived for dark mode rather than inverted. For contrast, WCAG 2.2 ratios remain the compliance bar. **APCA was removed from the WCAG 3 draft in July 2023**, and as of April 2026 the WCAG 3 contrast algorithm was still "yet to be determined" ([Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/04/wcag3-contrast-as-of-april-2026.html)).

Motion follows the same CSS-first pattern. Same-document View Transitions became **Baseline Newly available in October 2025** when Firefox 144 shipped ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)). `@starting-style` (Baseline 2024) handles dialog, popover and toast entry and exit ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@starting-style)). `linear()` (Baseline 2023) approximates spring curves in pure CSS ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/easing-function/linear)). Scroll-driven animation is **not Baseline**: it reaches about 88% of users globally, and Firefox gains it only at version 160 ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline_scroll)). It therefore belongs behind `@supports` on marketing pages only. Motion (formerly Framer Motion) handles shared-layout animation through WAAPI ([motion.dev](https://motion.dev/docs/react)), but for low-end Android it should be loaded only on the routes that need `layoutId`. Every animation should touch only `transform` and `opacity` ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). For shadows, the guidance is to keep blur small, avoid shadows on many elements, and never animate `box-shadow` itself, though the sources for that advice are old WebKit bug reports ([WebKit](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=22102)).

The accessibility floor is WCAG 2.2 AA, with the current edition dated 12 December 2024 ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). The criteria these aesthetics endanger most are 1.4.3 (text contrast, at risk from pastel clay), 1.4.11 (boundaries, at risk from neumorphism), 2.4.11 (focus not obscured, at risk from sticky headers and animated overlays) and 2.5.8 (24×24 CSS px targets in compact dashboards) ([Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Motion adds SC 2.2.2, which requires a pause control for auto-moving content that lasts longer than five seconds ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)), and AAA SC 2.3.3, which asks that interaction-triggered motion can be disabled through `prefers-reduced-motion` ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). A 2022 ACM paper found that Iran's regulations give accessibility insufficient attention ([RIT](https://www.rit.edu/academicaffairs/facultyscholarship/submit/download_file.php?id=126140)). Without a binding national standard, WCAG 2.2 AA plus 2.4.13 Focus Appearance (AAA) as an internal target is the defensible baseline.

## Persian RTL rewrites every borrowed trait

RTL is not a final mirroring pass. It changes which traits can be borrowed at all. **Vazirmatn** is OFL-licensed, ships a variable weight axis, and offers Non-Latin, Round-Dots and Farsi-Digits builds ([GitHub](https://github.com/rastikerdar/vazirmatn); [Fedora](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Vazirmatn_fonts)). Its author, Saber Rastikerdar, died in November 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_Rastikerdar)). That makes the font a maintenance risk worth tracking, with Estedad or Sahel as OFL fallbacks ([AUR](https://aur.archlinux.org/packages/estedad-fonts-git); [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/sahel-font)).

Persian text needs `letter-spacing: 0`, taller line-height than Latin, custom underlines that clear the dots, and `min-width` on buttons, because translated labels change length ([rtlstyling.com](https://rtlstyling.com/posts/rtl-styling)). It should not use two-margin justification, because browsers widen word spaces instead ([rgb.ir](https://rgb.ir/en/learn/graphic-design/persian-typography/)). The commonly used body line-height of about 1.7–1.9 has no authoritative source and should be tuned by testing. User-facing numbers should use Extended Arabic-Indic digits, U+06F0–06F9 ([W3C i18n](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-i18n-translation/2025AprJun/0009.html)). Generating them from data with `Intl.DateTimeFormat('fa-IR-u-ca-persian-nu-arabext')` keeps Jalali dates and digits consistent for copy-paste and screen readers ([lingo.dev](https://lingo.dev/en/javascript-i18n/format-dates-calendar-systems)). It has not been verified whether Vazirmatn supports `tnum` for aligned table columns, so test that before committing dashboard number styles.

Mirroring follows Material's bidirectionality rules. Directional icons, steppers, progress fills, drawers and view-transition slide direction mirror. Media playback, clockwise or clock icons, and numbers do not ([Material M2](https://m2.material.io/go/design-bidirectionality)). The trap specific to this synthesis is that **`box-shadow` offsets are physical**, so neumorphic, clay and neubrutalist shadows do not flip with `dir` ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/neumorphism-and-css/)). No published source settles which way a shadow should fall in RTL. The cleanest decision is a vertical-only light source (x = 0). It looks identical in Persian and in embedded English widgets and needs no direction token. For directional slides, which have no logical-axis variant of `translate`, a `--dir` sign variable (−1 under `[dir=rtl]`) handles the flip. User-generated marketplace text such as book titles and usernames needs `dir="auto"` or `<bdi>`.

| Neubrutalism | 2–3px solid outline as the focus, selected and error idiom; flat semantic colour without gradients; optional y-only hard shadow on community promo cards | Borders on every element; clashing saturated fills; quirky display type; retro and pixel nostalgia; hard shadows on static cards (false affordance) | Outlines satisfy 1.4.11 and 2.4.13; the attitude is advised against for finance and government |
| Kinetic typography | Word or line mask reveals and Vazirmatn weight morphs on hero and annual-impact pages; "data as kinetic copy" for impact reports | Per-letter spans; tracking animation; motion on labels, prices or legal text | Per-letter splitting breaks joining with no CSS fix |
| Soft UI / neumorphism | Single consistent light source; pressed state shown as inset or `scale(.98)`, always paired with a fill change and `aria-pressed` | Same-colour controls; shadow-only boundaries or states; animated `box-shadow` | Fails 1.4.11 structurally; shadows vanish in forced-colors mode |

The ship-ready shape is one primitive layer, one semantic layer and an intensity attribute. Primitives are OKLCH hue ramps: firouzeh as the interactive accent, lapis as brand, saffron and pomegranate as warm status colours, and a zero-chroma neutral ramp. Semantic tokens such as `--surface`, `--on-surface`, `--accent`, `--border-strong` and `--focus-ring` sit on `:root` and `[data-theme=dark]`, and are exposed to Tailwind through `@theme inline`. A `[data-intensity=productive|expressive]` attribute swaps only the tokens that should differ. The sketch below is a design proposal derived from the evidence above; its values are not taken from a source.

```css
:root {                                   /* shared by both intensities */
  --space-unit: 8px; --radius-control: 6px; --border-control: 1.5px;
  --focus-ring: 3px solid var(--accent-strong); /* neubrutalist outline idiom, ≥3:1 */
  --light-x: 0;                           /* vertical light: no RTL mirroring needed */
  --shadow-1: 0 1px 2px oklch(0% 0 0 / .12);
  --dur-fast: 70ms; --dur-1: 110ms; --dur-2: 150ms; --dur-3: 240ms; --dur-4: 400ms;
  --ease-productive: cubic-bezier(.2,0,.38,.9);
  --ease-expressive: cubic-bezier(.4,.14,.3,1);
}
[data-intensity=productive] {             /* dashboards, intake, booking, listings */
  --ease: var(--ease-productive); --type-heading: var(--type-fixed);
  --elevation-card: var(--shadow-1); --density: compact; /* targets stay ≥24px */
}
[data-intensity=expressive] {             /* home, blog, campaigns, onboarding */
  --ease: var(--ease-expressive); --type-heading: var(--type-fluid); /* clamp() */
  --elevation-hero-cta: 0 8px 16px oklch(0% 0 0 / .2),
    inset 0 -6px 10px oklch(0% 0 0 / .18), inset 0 6px 10px oklch(100% 0 0 / .35);
  --shape-organic-1: 30% 70% 70% 30% / 30% 30% 70% 70%;
}
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { :root { --dur-2: 0ms; --dur-3: 0ms; --dur-4: 0ms; } }
[dir=rtl] { --dir: -1; }  [dir=ltr] { --dir: 1; }
```

In practice the productive intensity looks like this. Dashboards, admin tables, insurance intake and consultation booking use the Swiss grid, bordered high-contrast inputs (the UK-government-forms model Malewicz praised in [Built In](https://builtin.com/design-ux/neumorphism-accessibility)), the outline focus ring, single-layer elevation, 70–150ms ease-out feedback, no decorative texture, explicit fees and statuses, and exactly one calm, fast confirmation at success instead of celebratory motion. The expressive intensity applies to the home page, blog, campaign and impact pages, and community marketplace headers. It uses fluid Vazirmatn display weights, garden photography, low-density tile geometry, clay illustration in empty and onboarding states, word-level kinetic headlines gated behind reduced-motion and `@supports`, and View Transitions for continuity. Every component must pass a forced-colors check, because shadows and background-only states disappear in that mode, so real or transparent borders are mandatory.

## Conclusion

The synthesis is easier than "eight styles" suggests, because the evidence sorts them. The two styles that define *order* are cheap, accessible and trust-aligned. Of the six that define *surface*, each contributes one or two traits, and only motion is mature enough to be tokenised across the whole product. The less obvious finding is that Persian-first RTL acts as a filter, not a finishing step. It removes per-letter kinetic type, tracked labels and mirrored physical shadows, and it pushes the system toward weight-based hierarchy and a vertical light source. Both choices also make the English secondary experience simpler. The chahar bagh, a geometric grid filled with planting, gives a culturally rooted name for the overall approach: Swiss structure with nature inside it, not a Western wellness aesthetic applied on top.

The biggest open risk is the lack of evidence about this specific audience. No source tests trust, motion tolerance or ornament preference with Iranian users, the Vazirmatn digit and `tnum` behaviour is unverified, and the font's maintainer has died. The first investments should therefore be a small preference-and-task study comparing productive-intensity forms with and without the expressive accents, including low-vision Persian readers, plus a font-maintenance contingency. Visual exploration should come after those.
