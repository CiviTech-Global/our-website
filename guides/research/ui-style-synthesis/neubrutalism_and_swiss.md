# Neubrutalism and Swiss Modernism (International Typographic Style) as source styles for the CiviTech Global design language

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: UI practice 2018–2026; classic sources for Swiss history. Every claim below has an inline source. Anything I could not source is in Inferences (labelled as reasoning) or Gaps. Several sources are secondary blogs or aggregators; their quality is noted where it matters.

## 1. Origins and history, and use in 2025–2026 digital products

### Takeaway
Swiss modernism is a 1940s–60s print movement from the Basel school (Hofmann, Ruder) and the Zurich school (Müller-Brockmann). It was built on the mathematical grid, flush-left sans type (Akzidenz-Grotesk, then Helvetica and Univers in 1957) and objective photography, and today it survives as the unstated basis of most grid-based design systems. Neubrutalism is a 2021–2024 web trend. It descends from "web brutalism" (brutalistwebsites.com, 2014) but is more colourful and orderly, and Gumroad (2021) and Figma made it popular. By 2024–2026 commentators describe it as saturated or fading at the leading edge (Figma reportedly moving on), while component libraries keep it alive in SaaS and indie products.

### Cited Findings
**Swiss / International Typographic Style**
- The style arose in Switzerland in the 1940s–50s. It favoured cleanliness, legibility and impartiality, and it grew out of Constructivist, De Stijl and Bauhaus ideas such as grids, sans-serif type and minimalism. — [Ulster Univ. IXD blog (student; secondary)](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/b00777404-ixd/?p=437); [Envato Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/what-is-swiss-design-and-why-you-should-know-its-history--cms-38463)
- Its technical hallmarks were a mathematical grid, sans-serif type set flush left and ragged right, and black-and-white photography in place of drawn illustration. — [Ulster Univ. IXD blog](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/b00813157-ixd/2022/01/11/international-typographic-style)
- Two schools refined it: Basel under Armin Hofmann (1920–2020) and Emil Ruder (1914–1970), and Zurich under Josef Müller-Brockmann (1914–1996). Ernst Keller at the Zurich Kunstgewerbeschule is called the "father of Swiss Design". Jan Tschichold's "Elemental Typography" and "The New Typography" were foundational. — [Envato Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/what-is-swiss-design-and-why-you-should-know-its-history--cms-38463); [Zürich Tourism, Swiss graphics](https://www.zuerich.com/en/art-culture/zurich-avantgarde/swiss-graphics-typography)
- Typeface lineage: Berthold's Akzidenz-Grotesk (1896) inspired Helvetica and Univers. Helvetica was released in 1957 as Neue Haas Grotesk by the Haas foundry (Max Miedinger and Eduard Hoffmann) and later renamed "Helvetica" ("Swiss" in Latin) for Linotype. Frutiger's Univers (1957) was among the first type families designed as a coordinated system of weights and widths. — [Artpublika, Helvetica history](https://www.artpublikamag.com/post/helvetica-a-brief-history-of-the-world-s-most-preferred-typeface); [Envato Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/what-is-swiss-design-and-why-you-should-know-its-history--cms-38463). Note: some sources credit Miedinger alone and omit Hoffmann (not to be confused with Armin Hofmann).
- Müller-Brockmann championed modular grids built on a basic repeating module that scales into hierarchical structures. His canonical text is *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* (1981), which warns that "The grid system is an aid, not a guarantee." — [UX Planet, "Swiss style web design"](https://uxplanet.org/swiss-style-web-design-everything-you-need-to-know-2b128fd327b9); [madegooddesigns, Swiss Design and the Grid](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=4700)
- Backlash: after 1968, International-Style advertising "came under fire as its cold, formal, dogmatic aesthetic was questioned". Wolfgang Weingart, who taught at Basel alongside Hofmann, broke the style apart from within in the 1970s ("New Wave" or "Swiss Punk"), and April Greiman carried that work to the US. — [Swiss National Library, "Postmodern posters 1970 to today"](https://www.nb.admin.ch/en/postmodern-posters-1970-to-today); [International Poster Gallery primer](https://internationalposter.com/style-primers/post-modernist-posters/)
- Public-sector descendant: since 2007 the Swiss Federal Administration has had a uniform "CD Bund" corporate design. Its Confederation Web Guidelines govern all admin.ch sites, make CD elements obligatory and unmodifiable, and ship standard HTML/CSS/JS templates. A newer system lives at swiss.github.io/designsystem. — [Swiss Confederation Web Guidelines README (npm swiss-styleguide 3.0.0)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/swiss-styleguide@3.0.0/README.md); [Liip, style guide for the Swiss Confederation](https://www.liip.ch/en/blog/style-guide-swiss-confederation)

**Web brutalism leading to neubrutalism**
- Pascal Deville, a Zurich creative director, launched brutalistwebsites.com in 2014. He described brutalism as "ruggedness and lack of concern to look comfortable or easy", said "Web-Brutalism is an attitude, not a visual style", and cited Craigslist as a commercially successful brutalist site. The term borrows from architectural béton brut. Attention went viral around 2016 via Hacker News. — [Seattle Web Design blog](https://www.seattlewebdesign.com/blog/seattle-web-design-online-marketing-blog/post/the-strange-new-web-design-trend-you-may-not-have-noticed); [Horizont (DE)](https://www.horizont.net/schweiz/nachrichten/Web-Design-Web-Brutalism-zeigt-was-digital-perfekt-unperfekt-ist-162453); [t3n (DE)](https://t3n.de/news/?p=705490); [Il Post 2016](https://www.ilpost.it/2016/05/13/siti-brutti-brutalismo/); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/web-brutalism/). Sources disagree on when attention peaked (2014 per Webflow, 2016 per LogRocket and Il Post).
- NN/g (Hayat Sheikh, 11 Apr 2025) defines neobrutalism as "a visual-design trend defined by high contrast, blocky layouts, bold colors, thick borders, and 'unpolished' elements". It is an evolution of brutalism that reacts against sleek minimalism, mixes in 1990s nostalgia (Win98-style buttons, pixel art) and is "more colorful and orderly" than true brutalism. NN/g's examples include Figma and Gumroad. — [NN/g, Neobrutalism](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/)
- Gumroad's 2021 rebrand under founder Sahil Lavingia introduced pink backgrounds, thick black outlines, flat pastel fills and buttons with hard offset shadows, under a "deliberately under-designed" philosophy. Reception was "polarizing": some users found it refreshing and others found it distracting. — [madegooddesigns, Neobrutalism (20 Mar 2026)](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/); [Medium, designstudiouiux](https://medium.com/@designstudiouiux/neo-brutalism-web-design-what-it-is-why-it-works-and-when-to-use-it-f5d7932fa8ec). A Medium claim that Gumroad surveys showed stronger brand recall has **no primary source**, so treat it as unverified.
- Conflict on Gumroad's current site: one 2026 review says Gumroad's current homepage no longer uses the technique. A design-system export ([webdesignhot Gumroad design.md](https://www.webdesignhot.com/api/design-md/gumroad/export/google-alpha)) still lists a hard black border and a `4px 4px 0 #000` hard shadow. I did not verify the live site.
- Figma was "among the first major design tools to incorporate neobrutalist elements into its brand and marketing", with thick-bordered cards, bold drop shadows and high-contrast colour blocks. — [madegooddesigns 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/). A 2024 UX Collective essay ("Foreshadowing Figma's next aesthetic ethos") reports that CEO Dylan Field said "Solarpunk is in", which suggests Figma moving past neubrutalism. — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/foreshadowing-figmas-next-aesthetic-ethos-1b982bd431e6) (403 on fetch; seen only as a search summary).
- Status in 2025–26: the style is common in SaaS landing pages (productivity, dev tools, creative software) and is packaged as open-source libraries. "Neobrutalism components" offers 64 components plus charts for React and Tailwind v4 on shadcn/ui and Base UI, with about 5.4k GitHub stars. BrutalistUI is another. madegooddesigns flags the risk that mainstreaming erodes its differentiation. — [neobrutalism.dev](https://www.neobrutalism.dev/); [madegooddesigns 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/)

### Inferences
- Swiss modernism is an underlying grammar (grid, hierarchy, neutrality) that most 2026 design systems absorbed without naming it. Neubrutalism is a surface skin with a datable trend cycle (2021 to about 2024 peak). That makes Swiss the safer foundation for a long-lived civic brand.
- Both styles have Zurich and "honesty" roots: Deville's brutalism and Swiss objectivity both value showing structure over decoration. That shared value ("show the structure") could be a bridge in the synthesis.

### Gaps
- I found no primary Gumroad or Figma design write-up (blog post or design-system doc) for the 2021 redesign. Attributions rest on secondary 2025–26 articles.
- I could not verify the current live state of gumroad.com or figma.com in 2026.
- No adoption statistics for either style were found.

## 2. Defining visual traits

### Takeaway
Swiss: modular grid, asymmetric layout, flush-left/ragged-right sans-serif type, hierarchy through size and weight, restrained palette with one accent (classically red, black and white), objective photography, generous white space. Neubrutalism: thick black borders, solid offset shadows with no blur, saturated or pastel flat fills, pure black next to colour, chunky display type over a neutral body face, blocky and often square shapes, retro and "unpolished" touches.

### Cited Findings
**Swiss**
- Elements sit on a mathematically constructed grid. Type is sans-serif and flush left, and compositions are asymmetric. — [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/swiss-style-web-design-everything-you-need-to-know-2b128fd327b9)
- Black-and-white photography replaces illustration, and the grid is mathematical. — [Ulster IXD](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/b00813157-ixd/2022/01/11/international-typographic-style)
- Typeface neutrality: Wim Crouwel said meaning lies "in the content of the text, not in the typeface". — [Artpublika](https://www.artpublikamag.com/post/helvetica-a-brief-history-of-the-world-s-most-preferred-typeface)

**Neubrutalism**
- Colour: bold primary colours and high-contrast pairings. Shapes: thick borders and angular forms, without gradients. Shadows: "solid, single-color offset shadows" instead of soft layered ones. Type: expressive, sometimes quirky, balanced by white space. Retro: Windows 98 buttons and pixel art. — [NN/g 2025](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/)
- Shadows must have no blur ("as sharp as possible"). Borders are bold and dark. The style is "not afraid to combine pure black (#000000) with other colors". — [SVGator](https://www.svgator.com/blog/what-is-neubrutalism/); [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/neo-brutalism)
- Starting values: 2–4px solid black borders on major elements. Offsets of about 4–8px x/y, no blur, filled black or accent. Adding border-radius or blurred shadows "softens" the style. — [madegooddesigns 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/)

### Inferences
- Motion: neither style has a sourced motion vocabulary. In practice neubrutalist buttons "press" by translating toward the shadow (the shadow shrinks), which is a mechanical, non-easing gesture (my synthesis, unsourced). Swiss motion in CSS has been explored as kinetic poster recreations, e.g. Jon Yablonski's CodePens of Müller-Brockmann posters — [Webdesigner Depot 2016](https://www.webdesignerdepot.com/2016/01/swiss-in-css-animating-the-international-typographic-style/).
- Radius: classic neubrutalism is square or near-square. Many 2023+ implementations (Gumroad, shadcn variants) use small radii, so "no radius" is a purist rule, not a universal one.

### Gaps
- No authoritative published palette spec for Gumroad or Figma's neubrutalist period was found.

## 3. CSS implementation techniques and token mapping

### Takeaway
Swiss maps onto CSS Grid (12-column `repeat(12, 1fr)` with asymmetric spans), an 8px-based spacing module, a modular type scale and logical alignment. IBM Carbon's 2x Grid is the best-documented production example of a Swiss-style modular grid. Neubrutalism reduces to a handful of tokens: border width and colour, shadow offset x/y and colour, radius, and a small set of flat fills.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 vanilla-CSS Swiss microsite uses a 12-column grid, asymmetric layouts and strict typographic rules with no framework. — [Danu Pratama, swiss-design](https://danu.is-a.dev/projects/swiss-design)
- IBM Carbon 2x Grid: the "8-pixel square mini unit" is the base, and all dimensions are multiples of it. Breakpoints are sm 320px (4 columns), md 672 (8), lg 1056 (16), xlg 1312 (16) and max 1584 (16). Padding is 16px at every breakpoint, margins run 0 to 24px, and the default gutter totals 32px; gutterless grids are allowed for closely related content. Type aligns to the box padding edge. Fixed sizing scale: 8/16/24/32/48/64/80px. Recommended aspect ratios: 1:1, 2:1, 2:3, 3:2, 4:3, 16:9. Fluid grids divide in halves and fixed grids multiply by 2. — [IBM Carbon, 2x Grid overview](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/2x-grid/overview/). The Carbon page itself does not cite Swiss design as inspiration.
- Neubrutalist shadow recipe seen in a Gumroad design export: `border: … solid #000` plus `box-shadow: 4px 4px 0 #000`. — [webdesignhot Gumroad design.md](https://www.webdesignhot.com/api/design-md/gumroad/export/google-alpha)
- Value ranges: border 2–4px, offset 4–8px, blur 0. — [madegooddesigns 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/)
- RTL-safe CSS uses logical properties: `text-align: start/end`, `padding-inline-start/end`, `margin-inline-start`, `border-inline-start` (e.g. an active-nav indicator) and `border-start-end-radius`. Mirror arrows with `transform: scaleX(-1)`, and provide physical-property fallbacks. — [rtlstyling.com, Ahmad Shadeed](https://rtlstyling.com/posts/rtl-styling)

### Inferences
These are my synthesis, not from the sources.
- Suggested token set:
  - `--border-w: 2px`, `--border-color`
  - `--shadow-x: 4px`, `--shadow-y: 4px`, `--shadow-color`
  - `--radius: 0 | 4px`
  - `--grid-cols: 12` (or Carbon-style 4/8/16), `--space-unit: 8px`
  - A modular type scale such as 1.25 for UI and 1.333 for marketing
- **`box-shadow` has no logical-property equivalent, so offset x does not flip with `dir`.** For RTL you need `--shadow-x` set negative under `[dir="rtl"]`, e.g. `box-shadow: calc(var(--shadow-x) * var(--dir-sign)) var(--shadow-y) 0 var(--shadow-color)`, or use a symmetric (y-only) shadow that needs no mirroring. The same applies to `translate()` "press" animations.
- Neubrutalist hover/press uses `transform: translate(var(--shadow-x), var(--shadow-y)); box-shadow: none;` (common pattern, unsourced). Guard it with `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the token definitions from neobrutalism.dev; its /styling page renders them client-side.
- No source explicitly documents mirroring offset shadows in RTL. rtlstyling.com only covers box-shadow for underline fallbacks. This is a genuine gap in the published guidance, and the recommendation above is reasoning.
- The Material Design bidirectionality pages (m2 and m3) rendered empty to the fetcher, so their mirroring rules are not cited here.

## 4. Documented strengths

### Takeaway
Swiss: legibility, objectivity and neutrality, systematic hierarchy, scalability through modular grids, and suitability for institutional identity (CD Bund). Neubrutalism: brand distinctiveness and personality, strong contrast, obvious element boundaries, attention on calls to action, and an "honest, under-designed" signal.

### Cited Findings
- Swiss: valued "cleanliness, legibility and impartiality" — [Ulster IXD](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/b00777404-ixd/?p=437). Univers's family system allowed one style to work at many sizes and weights — [Artpublika](https://www.artpublikamag.com/post/helvetica-a-brief-history-of-the-world-s-most-preferred-typeface). Modular grids scale into hierarchical structures — [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/swiss-style-web-design-everything-you-need-to-know-2b128fd327b9).
- Swiss in government: CD Bund "promotes the recognizability of federal websites and prevents costly uncontrolled growth in web design" — [Swiss Confederation Web Guidelines](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/swiss-styleguide@3.0.0/README.md).
- Neubrutalism: bold colours and high contrast "draw attention to key elements", and size and colour intensity keep CTAs prominent — [NN/g 2025](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/). Bold styling can help accessibility but "adds a few more objects for our brains to process" — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/neo-brutalism).
- Brutalism's honesty: Deville framed it as exposing construction, and called Craigslist brutalist and commercially very successful — [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/?p=705490).

### Inferences
- For a civic platform, Swiss strengths (neutrality, recognisability, hierarchy) line up with trust. Neubrutalism's strengths (personality, CTA salience) line up with marketplaces and marketing.

### Gaps
- No controlled usability or conversion studies comparing either style were found.

## 5. Documented weaknesses and accessibility implications

### Takeaway
Neubrutalism risks visual overload, unclear hierarchy, ambiguous controls and failing colour pairs. It is explicitly advised against for finance, healthcare, government and luxury. Swiss has been criticised historically as cold, formal and dogmatic. Its accessibility risks come from execution (light-weight type, low-contrast greys, removed affordances), not from the principles themselves.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g: neobrutalism can overwhelm users and hurt accessibility if unbalanced, and "success hinges on balancing boldness with usability". Bold colours must still meet contrast standards; avoid pairs like yellow on cyan. Limit the palette to two or three bold colours. Use a neutral body font, never decorative or condensed faces for paragraphs. Pad generously around thick borders. Signal hover and press with underline or colour shifts. — [NN/g 2025](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/)
- Poorly done hierarchies become a problem, and overly chaotic designs "can cause anxiety". — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/neo-brutalism)
- An accessibility commenter says the style makes it hard to tell UI controls apart and to indicate differences, and that it translates poorly to mobile touch. — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/neo-brutalism) (quoted commentary)
- **Avoid neobrutalism for:** luxury brands, healthcare/medical ("users may be anxious or vulnerable"), financial services ("rely on signals of stability"), and government/institutional sites ("diverse audiences needing neutral clarity"). It also has keyboard and screen-reader concerns. — [madegooddesigns, 20 Mar 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/)
- Swiss coldness: "cold, formal, dogmatic aesthetic" (post-1968 critique) — [Swiss National Library](https://www.nb.admin.ch/en/postmodern-posters-1970-to-today). Weingart: "the conservative dogma of 1950s Swiss typography stifled my playful, inquisitive, experimental temperament" — [International Poster Gallery](https://internationalposter.com/style-primers/post-modernist-posters/).
- Trust context (indirect): a 2026 study found perceived ease of use mediates the link between interface quality and intention in fintech, and that trust drives attitudes. — [VilniusTech BMEE journal](https://journals.vilniustech.lt/index.php/BMEE/article/download/24000/13253). It does not test aesthetics specifically.

### Inferences
- Thick black borders on every element compete with the semantic borders a dashboard needs (selected row, error field, focus ring). If everything is outlined, outlines stop signalling state.
- Hard offset shadows look like "pressable" affordances. Applied to non-interactive cards, they create false affordances, which matters for forms.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found measuring perceived trust for neubrutalist vs neutral UI in insurance, government or finance. The "unprofessional in trust contexts" claim rests on practitioner opinion (madegooddesigns 2026, NN/g's implied caution).
- The GOV.UK focus-states page (yellow #ffdd00 focus with a thick black underline/outline, widely cited) could not be fetched (socket errors), so its exact values are **not cited here**. Verify at https://design-system.service.gov.uk/get-started/focus-states/.

## 6. Suitability: dashboards, marketing pages, forms, product cards

### Takeaway
Swiss principles suit data-dense dashboards, tables and forms: the grid, alignment and hierarchy through weight and size carry most of the load. Neubrutalism suits marketing heroes, empty states, marketplace product cards and promo moments. Applied across dense dashboards it creates noise.

### Cited Findings
- Carbon's 2x Grid, which drives IBM's dense enterprise product UIs, allows gutterless grids for closely related content and keeps 16px padding everywhere. — [IBM Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/2x-grid/overview/)
- NN/g urges "ample whitespace" and generous padding around thick borders — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/). That conflicts with information density.
- Neubrutalism is typically found on SaaS landing pages (productivity, dev tools, creative software) — [madegooddesigns 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/).

### Inferences
- Dashboards and tables: Swiss (hairline 1px rules, no shadows, tabular numerals, start-aligned text, numerals aligned consistently).
- Forms: Swiss layout with high-visibility outlines borrowed from brutalism, i.e. 2px input borders that read clearly against the background, and a strong focus ring.
- Marketplace product cards (books, shops, freelance): a restrained neubrutalist accent may work, such as a single offset shadow on hover or a selected state. Avoid saturated multi-colour fills on cards that carry prices.
- Marketing and blog: the most licence for expressive type and blocky colour fields, but keep them off insurance-intake and consultation-booking flows.

### Gaps
- No sourced case study of neubrutalism applied to an admin dashboard was found.

## 7. RTL / Persian and Arabic-script considerations

### Takeaway
Swiss principles carry over to RTL, but you must mirror them: grid start and asymmetry run from the right, and text is flush-right/ragged-left. Arabic script has no capitals, italics or small caps, so hierarchy has to come from weight, size and proportion, which variable Vazirmatn supports. Persian needs zero letter-spacing, a taller line-height than Latin, custom underlines (dots), and consistent use of one numeral system (Persian digits U+06F0–06F9 per CLDR `fa`). Offset shadows don't flip automatically.

### Cited Findings
- Suisse Int'l Arabic (Pascal Zoghbi with Swiss Typefaces) pairs Latin geometric proportions and low-contrast strokes with humanist Naskh features. "Due to the absence of italics, caps, and small caps in the Arabic script, weight and proportional variations are crucial for creating complex visual hierarchy." — [Typographica review](https://typographica.org/?p=22377)
- Khatt Foundation (Amsterdam, 2004, Huda Smitshuijzen AbiFarès) aims at "modernising the image of Arabic design… particularly in the field of bilingual typography". It runs the Typographic Matchmaking series (2008–2017) and Kitabat (Dubai, 2006). — [Wikipedia, Khatt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatt_Foundation); [khtt.net About](https://www.khtt.net/en/page/373/about-the-khatt-foundation)
- Arabic needs `letter-spacing: 0` because tracking breaks joins. It needs its own, taller line-height; a tight line-height on a Twitter button clipped a kasra. Default underlines collide with dots, so use custom underlines or `text-decoration-skip-ink`. Keep numerals consistent (one system). Translated labels vary in length (LinkedIn's "Done" became "تم"), so set `min-width` on buttons. Some Arabic fonts' bold weights hurt readability. — [rtlstyling.com](https://rtlstyling.com/posts/rtl-styling)
- Icon mirroring: flip directional icons (breadcrumb arrows, tabs, blockquote). Don't flip media playback (it represents tape direction) or objects held in the right hand. Send icons are debated. — [rtlstyling.com](https://rtlstyling.com/posts/rtl-styling)
- Persian locales call for Extended Arabic-Indic digits (U+06F0–U+06F9). Forcing Western digits conflicts with CLDR `fa`/`fa-IR`. Persian's baseline and ascender proportions differ from Latin, which causes uneven mixed-script line heights. Vazirmatn is cited as the Persian font. — [W3C i18n list, May 2025 (one contributor's argument)](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-i18n-translation/2025AprJun/0009.html); [W3C i18n PR](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-i18n-archive/2025AprJun/0131.html)
- A design-system document recommends Vazirmatn (full Unicode, variable weight axis), a Tahoma fallback, logical `start/end` alignment and 45–75 characters per line. — [nick-doc Typography.md (informal)](https://git.manko.yoga/nick/nick-doc/raw/branch/main/05%20-%20Design%20System/Typography.md)

### Inferences
- Swiss "flush-left/ragged-right" becomes **flush-right/ragged-left (start-aligned)** in Persian. Asymmetric layouts should anchor to the inline-start (right) edge. Using CSS Grid with logical placement (`grid-column` with `dir="rtl"`) handles this automatically, because grid lines number from the inline start.
- Swiss practice of uppercase tracked labels has no Persian equivalent. Replace it with weight (Vazirmatn 600–800) or size steps, or a tinted label colour. Never letter-space Persian.
- Hard offset shadows: classic neubrutalism casts down-right, as if light comes from the upper-left (reading start in LTR). Mirroring to down-left in RTL keeps the "light from reading start" logic. Because `box-shadow` is physical, this needs an explicit direction token. Alternatively, a y-only shadow avoids the question. Product decision; no source found.
- Mixed numerals in tables: choose Persian digits for user-facing content consistently, and use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` (if Vazirmatn supports tnum, unverified) for column alignment.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced body of work explicitly applying the Swiss grid to Persian or Arabic layouts (e.g. a history of Arabic modernist grids). Khatt Foundation publications (e.g. AbiFarès, *Arabic Typography: A Comprehensive Sourcebook*) are likely sources but were not fetched.
- I found no recommended numeric line-height for Persian/Vazirmatn body text.
- Material Design's bidirectionality guidance could not be retrieved (both m2 and m3 pages rendered empty).
- I could not verify whether Vazirmatn supports OpenType `tnum`.

## 9. What to borrow and what to reject for a trust-sensitive civic platform

### Takeaway
Borrow from Swiss: the modular grid (mirrored for RTL), a strict type scale with hierarchy through weight and size, restrained palette plus one accent, start-aligned text, objective imagery, generous but systematic spacing. Borrow from neubrutalism, selectively: visible high-contrast outlines (inputs, focus rings, selected states), honest flat colour without gradients, and possibly one hard-shadow accent reserved for primary CTAs or marketplace cards. Reject: neubrutalism everywhere (thick borders on every element, multiple clashing saturated fills, quirky display type in flows, decorative retro elements, shadows on non-interactive surfaces), and Swiss coldness taken to sterility (all-grey, tiny light-weight type, removed affordances).

### Cited Findings
- Practitioner advice: avoid neobrutalism for financial, healthcare and government contexts — [madegooddesigns 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/neobrutalism-web-design/).
- Limit to two or three bold colours, keep body type neutral, and check contrast — [NN/g 2025](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/).
- Swiss neutrality is the basis of institutional recognisability (CD Bund) — [Swiss Confederation guidelines](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/swiss-styleguide@3.0.0/README.md).
- Arabic-script hierarchy must come from weight and proportion — [Typographica](https://typographica.org/?p=22377).

### Inferences
- **Safe to borrow:**
  - 12/16-column grid on an 8px module (Carbon-style), mirrored for RTL
  - Vazirmatn weight-based hierarchy
  - 2px solid borders on form controls and data containers
  - A 3px+ high-contrast focus outline
  - Flat semantic colours (success, warning, error) with no gradients
  - One brand accent
  - Hairline table rules
- **Use sparingly (marketing, marketplace, blog only):**
  - Offset hard shadow (`4px 4px 0`, mirrored in RTL) on primary CTAs or hover states of product cards
  - Blocky colour sections in hero areas
- **Reject for insurance intake, consultation booking and admin:**
  - Neubrutalist thick-border-everywhere
  - Clashing saturated fills
  - Quirky or condensed display fonts in body text
  - Press-translate animations without reduced-motion guards
  - Win98 or pixel nostalgia
  - All-caps conventions (meaningless in Persian)

### Gaps
- No user research on Persian-speaking audiences' trust perception of either aesthetic was found. This needs primary research (e.g. preference or trust tests with Iranian users).
